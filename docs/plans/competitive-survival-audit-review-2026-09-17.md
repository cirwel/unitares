## Where this review disagrees with the audit

Stated first so they survive the detail.

1. **The dichotomy in claim 1 is false.** A system can be overgrown and unnecessary at once. The audit's evidence for "not unnecessary" is a candidate wedge it calls unvalidated. The honest state is: overgrowth verified, necessity untested.
2. **The audit misdiagnoses the weakest component.** Self-reporting is not the property that makes the policy path weak; in-band signals, an empty outcome channel, and an unvalidated estimator that is unconditional on every check-in are. The audit's own companion, #2255, feeds that estimator more in-band signal and adds a tool-boundary gate that extends its reach. #2255 and #2257 pull in opposite directions on the component #2257 calls weakest, and neither says so.
3. **The audit misidentifies the strongest neighbor of the review record.** For coding agents the pull-request thread already preserves disagreement with conditions, enforces them through branch protection, and has total distribution. The wedge is therefore narrower than "disagreement-preserving record": disagreement before a pull request exists, outside code, and between agents.
4. **"Underlie" is the wrong verb for Relay and the cloud planes.** Relay is pre-1.0 and single-vendor; the cloud planes are not self-hostable. The product's one hard rule is that the core runs free and self-hosted. Adapters, yes; substrate, no.
5. **The present-tense federation claim the audit wants removed does not exist.** Every reader-facing surface already qualifies cross-operator federation as research. The audit should cite the sentence or drop the row.
6. **The audit's stop list omits the largest platform investment.** Six Elixir applications totalling about 42,000 lines, a signed Wave 3 go-decision, an implemented-but-undeployed message transport, and an unsigned wake gate are the "broad federation platform" in practice. The stop list names copy and generic features.
7. **The audit drops outcomes from the product and adds retrieval.** Outcome binding is the only mechanism that makes a claim gradable; it is the audit's own keep item 4 and #2256's second-strongest row. Retrieval is the read side of claims and reconstruction, not a workflow.
8. **The 30-day test's control arm is artisanal.** A hand-written structured handoff is not the standard stack. The standard stack is Git, the pull-request thread, CI, and the harness's own session transcript. The test also conflates capture with retrieval, which the repository's own two-stage rehearsal design already separates.
9. **The audit has no demand-side analysis and no harness vendor in its competitor list.** Both matter, and both cut against the product: the regulatory driver is weaker than it looks, and the harness vendors are absorbing the trace and attribution half of the record.
10. **The audit is a governed agent auditing its governor, and so is this review.** Both were prompted by the same operator and read the same documents. The threat model names this channel ("the governor is authored by the governed"); it applies to audits as well as diffs. Independence requires a reader outside the fleet, which is what the empty enrollment ledger says the project does not have.

## Claim by claim

### Claim 1 — "UNITARES is overgrown rather than fundamentally unnecessary." Verdict: **modify**

**What the repository shows.** The audit's own counts (42 tools, 13,614 test functions, 67 migrations, 524 modules) come from `docs/EVIDENCE_AND_LIMITS.md` and describe the Python server. They undercount the growth. Not in the audit's numbers: six Elixir applications under `elixir/` (lease plane 17,596 lines, sentinel 13,450, agent orchestrator 4,966, wave3a handlers 2,879, dialectic_live 2,128, SDK 1,378; about 42,400 lines in total); 95 top-level proposal documents plus 20 resolved (the index's own live tags: Built 21, Registered 7, Active 26, Parked 24, Closed 14); 169 documented environment flags in `docs/FLAGS.md`; a 70-entry legacy alias table; a 2,424-line changelog; and an evidence apparatus that has outrun its own capacity to execute: seven registered protocols, and at least five preregistrations that cannot currently run (the independent-operator cohort with an empty enrollment ledger, the EISV incremental-value ablation with collection disabled, the accountable-coordination ablation registered 2026-09-15 with no cohort, the KG agent-adoption pilot on HOLD, the relational-calibration pilot with runtime collection blocked). The growth is not mainly in the catalog. It is in substrate migration, coordination machinery, and governance of the governance.

**Where this review differs.** "Rather than" asserts a choice the evidence does not support. Whether the product is necessary to anyone is the untested question, and the audit says so in its own confidence line. The one piece of value evidence found in this review is real but awkward: the knowledge graph holds a run of cross-vendor coordination notes between Claude and Codex sessions on an unrelated project, one of which records that "this exchange ran with zero operator relay." That is the shared record doing useful work. It is also the misuse of the knowledge graph as a message bus that `docs/proposals/agent-message-transport-v0.md` documents (45 of 59 channel notes still open on 2026-08-28), and it is dogfood. Necessity remains untested.

**What would change this reading.** One external operator enrollment, or a capture-stage result from a real incident (see the test section below).

### Claim 2 — "Its weakest component is governance derived from agent self-reporting." Verdict: **modify**

**What the repository shows.** The weak component is correctly located and wrongly explained.

- The estimator is unconditional. Every `sync_state` runs the EISV update at `src/mcp_handlers/updates/phases.py` (the `process_update_authenticated_async` call inside `execute_locked_update` has no bypass; exceptions re-raise). None of the 169 flags disables it; the closest are within-EISV switches (`UNITARES_PHI_TELEMETRY_ONLY`, the coherence-gate and grounding shadows). The persisted row, `core.agent_state`, is an EISV row by schema.
- It is the only thing that gates writes. `AGENT_PAUSED` refuses the check-in and the knowledge-graph `store` and `note` actions (`check_agent_can_operate` has exactly two call sites in `src/mcp_handlers/knowledge/handlers.py`). `update_finding`, `export`, `dialectic`, `outcome_event`, and `identity` are not gated.
- Its evidence status is the ledger's: predictive lift is a non-detection whose preserved record lacks the cluster geometry to reconstruct read-specific power ([power audit](../operations/falsifiability-power-audit-2026-08-23.md)); pause delivery was suppressed for 195 of 218 recorded pauses at the 2026-08-06 audit; a knowledge-graph note from the 2026-05-30 database audit (status superseded) reads "fleet ~99% ODE-fallback, verdict ~12% recall, external ground-truth plumbing empty." The probe in this review confirmed the shape: one check-in returned `action: proceed` on `evidence_basis: ode_fallback`, `corroboration: claim_only`, and minted a `prediction_id` anyway.

**Where this review differs.** Self-report is not the cause. The behavioral path takes server-side signals (tool usage, continuity metrics, calibration and outcome history) and the plugin's per-turn check-ins arrive as `epistemic_class="substrate_interpretation"`, a substrate reading rather than an agent-authored report. The weaknesses are (a) every signal is in-band, which is the calibrated-concealment blind spot the threat model already names; (b) the outcome channel is nearly empty (17 joinable outcome rows in `docs/proposals/eisv-stage0-bridge-b-label-routing.md`; `reviewer_provenance` has never been populated; `audit.outcome_events` carries EISV columns but no foreign key to any claim; `audit.outcome_prediction_bindings` omits referential constraints by design); and (c) the governor is authored by the governed, which the threat model rates as cheaper than gaming any self-report.

The diagnosis matters because the two readings prescribe opposite fixes. If self-report is the weakness, the fix is to feed the estimator signals from elsewhere, which is what the Relay exporter in #2255 does, and to enforce its verdicts at the tool boundary, which #2255's policy gate does. If the weakness is an unvalidated estimator on the gating path, the fix is to take the estimator off the gating path by default until the pre-registered read, which is a configuration default and not an integration. #2257 and #2255 therefore pull in opposite directions on the same component, and the audit does not mention it.

One more correction: self-reporting is not confined to the governance side. `record_result` from the reporting agent is an outcome claim, not independent evidence (the threat model says so), and the outcome tables carry no producer-identity constraint. The record has the same exposure as the estimator.

### Claim 3 — "Its strongest component is an operator-owned, disagreement-preserving accountability record." Verdict: **modify**

**What the repository shows.** The review record is real. A standing reviewer objection blocks resolution and reassignment cannot erase it (`DialecticSession._reviewer_objection_stands` in `src/dialectic_protocol.py`); 81 non-canary verdicts, 77 carrying conditions, 42 dissents since the 2026-07-02 reviewer-label split; conditions imposed at resolution are enforced on later check-ins (`src/mcp_handlers/dialectic/enforcement.py`: complexity limits, risk targets, monitoring windows). No source opened in this review or in #2256 combines attributed claims, standing objections with enforced conditions, and prediction-bound outcomes under a process identity.

**Where this review differs.**

- *Strongest engineering versus strongest bet.* As engineering, the most mature components are the identity write gate and the trust-contract discipline (`measured`, `derived`, `prior`, `unknown`; lint-enforced; zero-observation honesty tests; the claim ledger). The review record's engineering is uneven: `src/mcp_handlers/dialectic/` is about 9,100 lines with a 4,045-line handler module; 31 historical resolutions are not provably bilateral; the deployment receipt is dormant; and the link from a dialectic session to the disputed finding (`core.dialectic_sessions.discovery_id`) is a text column with no foreign key, so the claim-to-challenge edge is a string. The `Resolution` payload stores merged conditions, root cause, and combined reasoning; dissent survives in the message transcript and the standing-objection state, not as a minority report in the resolution.
- *The strongest neighbor is misnamed.* The audit lists "human review systems, workflow engines." For coding agents the pull-request thread is the disagreement-preserving record: a "changes requested" review is a standing objection, branch protection enforces it, and the thread is retained with the code. GitHub's agentic audit events now carry an agent session identifier and the initiating human, and agent-authored commits link to their session log **[unverified]**. Against that neighbor the wedge is narrower than the audit states: disagreement raised mid-task before any pull request exists; disagreement about work that is not code (research, operations, residents); and disagreement between agents. That is still a wedge. It should be named at its real width.
- *Which adjective carries the weight.* "Operator-owned" is not what distinguishes the record; GitHub, Codex, and Claude Code all keep operator-visible logs. "Vendor-neutral and cross-harness" is (as #2256 row 9 already says).

### Claim 5 — "UNITARES should stop presenting itself as a broad federation platform." Verdict: **agree on the word, reject the finding**

**What the repository shows.** `docs/PRODUCT_DEFINITION.md` defines federation as "many independent runtimes and harnesses sharing one operator-controlled server and authority domain" and explicitly disclaims owning execution or replicating state. `docs/public-site/index.md` carries a section titled "Cross-operator federation is a research direction." `docs/EVIDENCE_AND_LIMITS.md` names the symmetric-attestation blocker. A search of every reader-facing surface for cross-operator, between-operator, or multi-operator wording finds only those qualifications. The audit's matrix row "Cross-operator federation: Absent/blocked; remove from present-tense product positioning" removes nothing, because nothing is there. Cite the sentence or drop the row.

**Where this review agrees.** The word should go, for two reasons the audit gives only half of. First, "federation kernel" reads as a claim on cross-runtime interoperability, which A2A now owns (#2256 row 2). Second, the merged presentation critique counts ten different one-sentence definitions live across the site, the repository, and the share cards; readers cannot tell which one is the definition. The fix is editorial and already in flight: one sentence, owned in one place, copied verbatim everywhere. The audit's seven-day action 2 (a separate positioning PR) duplicates work that #2251 recommends and #2256 drafts, on a single-writer surface; one session should land it.

**Where the platform story really lives.** In code, not copy: the Elixir lease plane and orchestrator, the governed-effect execute plane (off by default, three flags), the message transport with its unapplied migration, the wake gate, and the Wave 3 substrate commitment signed GO-WITH-REDUCED-SCOPE on 2026-08-22. The audit's stop list names "general agent orchestration, model routing, inference hosting, or agent framework features" and does not name these surfaces. #2255's packet asks the operator three parking questions about exactly them (hook chain, execute half, lease plane) and explicitly does not reopen the Wave 3 decision. The audit should reference that packet rather than restate a stop list that leaves the largest surface unnamed.

### Claim 6 — "EISV should become an optional research module rather than the product foundation." Verdict: **modify**

**What the repository shows.** In prose EISV has already been demoted: "runtime proprioception" in the README, "background proprioception rather than a product-facing judgment" in the KG adoption pilot decision of 2026-08-27, excluded from arm assignment and outcome labelling in the accountable-coordination ablation. In code it is the foundation: the check-in cannot run without it; there is no kill switch; `core.agent_state` is EISV; `export` exports the EISV trajectory and nothing else ("the monitor's retained rolling history, not the append-only audit record"); `audit.outcome_events` carries E, I, S, V, phi, verdict, coherence, and regime inline; about 56 modules and 15,700 lines in the fifteen largest EISV files. The documents say the state estimate interprets the record; the schema says the record is the state estimate. That gap is a finding in its own right.

**Where this review agrees.** EISV should not be the foundation of the accountability product, and no new EISV product features should land before the evidence gates resolve. The repository's momentum already contradicts the freeze the audit proposes: #2250 (a replay-only constraint-pressure primitive) merged on 2026-09-15, and #2255 extends the estimator's reach into Relay.

**Where this review differs.**

- *"Optional module" is the wrong mechanism.* The check-in is the only automatic capture the harness performs (the plugin's `post-stop` files one per turn). Make EISV optional and the check-in goes with it; the record then depends on agents volunteering findings, which is the failure mode the design-rationale literature predicts (see the missed-assumptions section). Decouple instead: keep the check-in as a checkpoint record, keep the prediction identifier that outcome binding grades (that depends on the check-in's shape, not on the E/I/S/V computation), make the verdict advisory by default, and unwire the pause gate from knowledge-graph `store` in new installs. That is a default change and a schema seam, not a module extraction.
- *Timing.* The 2026-12-01 outcome-grounding read is pre-registered, and the shared contract forbids weakening, re-running, or refreshing it. Rewiring the instrument inside the read window is instrument drift. Structural extraction waits until after the read or runs shadow-only.
- *The research module is the ODE, not EISV as a whole.* The free-energy formulation is already diagnostic-only. The behavioral path is a deployed heuristic. The paper's title ("Information-Theoretic Governance") should follow the demotion (#2256 row 15); that is the repository outside this one.

### Claim 7 — "The product should contract toward roughly five workflows: identity, retrieval, claims/evidence, challenge/correction, and reconstruction." Verdict: **modify**

**What the repository shows.** `docs/PRODUCT_DEFINITION.md` already states the product as five questions with one deployed mechanism each: who said it, what supports it, who challenged it, what happened, what can a successor recover. The audit's list is that definition with one substitution: outcomes out, retrieval in. The catalog does not look like either list: 42 tools, eight routers over 52 actions, eight workflow aliases, and a 70-entry legacy alias table, which is evidence that the surface has been renamed more often than reduced.

**Where this review differs.**

- *Outcomes are a workflow.* Binding a prediction to what later happened is the only mechanism that turns a claim into a gradable one; it is the audit's own keep item 4, the second-strongest row in #2256, and the thing that makes the record more than a flight recorder. It is also now exactly-once (#2246). Dropping it from the five is a regression.
- *Retrieval is not a workflow.* It is the read side of claims (search before write) and of reconstruction. Listing it separately hides that reconstruction has no tool: the server's own instructions say "there is no single reconstruction tool," and reconstruction is assembled from `knowledge` reads, `dialectic` reads, `observe(audit_events, outcome_evidence)`, and an `export` that does not contain claims.
- *The check-in must survive contraction.* Not as a verdict, as the checkpoint that captures automatically (claim 6 above).
- *What contraction actually requires.* Four tables in three schemas joined only by agent id and time, with no foreign key crossing them (`knowledge.discoveries`, `core.dialectic_sessions`, `audit.outcome_events`, `audit.events`); the `discovery_id` on a dialectic session declared without a constraint; the prediction-binding table without one by design. The audit's seven-day action 3, one portable accountability bundle linking claims, evidence, objections, conditions, outcomes, and source artifact ids, is the right first artifact. It should be the only build item, because every other item on the list (the test, the export, the A2A seam, the neutral rendering readers need) depends on it.

## The independent questions

### What UNITARES provides that the systems named do not

Stated at the width the evidence supports.

1. **Process-instance identity with declared causal lineage, enforced as a write gate across heterogeneous harnesses.** A fresh process gets a fresh identity; lineage is a pointer to inherited work, never inheritance of authority. Every platform opened uses "agent identity" to mean a credential (Entra Agent ID, SPIFFE-based identity, AgentCore Identity). None states this contract.
2. **Reviewer conditions that bind on the reviewed process's later checkpoints, and a standing objection that reassignment cannot erase.** Annotation queues (Langfuse, LangSmith, Braintrust) record a named reviewer's score and comment on a trace; A2A has an `INPUT_REQUIRED` state; none imposes and enforces conditions on subsequent work.
3. **Prediction-to-outcome binding at the check-in level, exactly once.** Evaluation platforms score outputs; the IETF audit-trail draft records an action's outcome; neither binds a later outcome to an earlier self-assessment.
4. **Provenance-labelled emissions with a lint** (`measured`, `derived`, `prior`, `unknown`) for the monitor's own outputs. Not a competitive capability; a trust property no neighbor states.
5. **A vendor-neutral, self-hosted record spanning Claude Code, Codex, and custom runtimes, with ten months of continuous operation and a published dataset.** The harness vendors' records are per-harness and retained on their terms (GitHub agentic audit events 180 days, Codex compliance logs 30 days, Claude Code transcripts 30 days locally by default, Managed Agents server-side) **[unverified]**.
6. **An evidence discipline** (claim ledger with evidence classes, preregistrations with stop rules, published negative results). A property of the project, not the product, and rarer than any feature.

What it implies but does not provide: a unified claim object; a reconstruction read; a tamper-evident or standard-format export (#2256 gap 1); attestations another operator can verify.

### Retain, integrate, freeze, retire

Retirement candidates are listed by redundancy or cost, never by a usage count, and every disposition is the operator's decision. "Freeze" means flag-off with documents retained and no new work, the audit's own meaning.

| Disposition | Surface | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Retain and concentrate** | Identity write gate and lineage; findings with `supersede` and provenance; the dialectic record, the standing objection, and conditions enforcement; outcome binding; the trust-contract lint; the check-in as a checkpoint record | The five questions, plus the one automatic capture path. |
| **Build (the single build item)** | One portable accountability bundle with real links between claim, evidence, objection, condition, and outcome | Prerequisite for the export, the test, and any neutral rendering a cold reader can be blind to. |
| **Integrate (adapters, both directions)** | OpenTelemetry GenAI exporter; IETF Agent Audit Trail export (#2256); A2A for messaging with `INPUT_REQUIRED` as the pause seam; NeMo Relay as one ingest and enforcement adapter behind the same interface as the hook chain (#2255); optional external-principal binding; Cedar (existing proposal) for authorization; trace platforms as sinks | Standards where they exist; one adapter interface so no vendor is the substrate. |
| **Enable** | The Ed25519 deployment receipt (`drr.v1`), dormant | The smallest change with the largest effect on the federation blocker. |
| **Freeze** | BEAM Wave 3 handler dispatch (the signed go-decision is not relitigated here; the audit should at least name it as the largest platform investment and ask for it to be reaffirmed or parked in light of Relay); the governed-effect execute half; the agent orchestrator (dormant); the agent message transport, wake gate, and send/inbox verbs (superseded by A2A); the ODE and free-energy path (diagnostic only until the December read); PCALM (replay only); the hosted multi-tenant endpoint; the resident runtime; new preregistrations until one existing protocol enrolls | Each is substrate, transport, or evidence apparatus that the narrow product does not need and that a standard now covers. |
| **Retire candidates (by redundancy)** | The 70-entry legacy alias table; duplicated EISV shapes in responses (trust contract §6 row 6, open); the resident-roster paragraph in every non-roster `start_session` response | Surface legibility; none of these is a capability. |

### Would the reviewing session use it — a first-hand probe

The session onboarded as an ordinary agent under the shared contract and used the record the way the contract asks. What happened:

- `start_session(force_new=true)`: worked. The `minimal` envelope was 3.7 KB and included a four-line paragraph about resident-roster registration that applies to no ordinary agent. The identity tier was `weak` until the session id was passed back, which is documented and correct.
- `search_shared_memory`, twice, on the subjects of this review: the first query returned only low-confidence semantic matches, all cross-vendor channel chatter about an unrelated project; the second returned eight, showed three, of which two were channel chatter and one was a genuinely relevant but superseded finding (the 2026-05-30 database audit). None of the September competitive work (the audit, #2255, #2256, the presentation critique) exists in the knowledge graph. The project's own knowledge lives in `docs/` and pull requests; the shared memory holds mostly messages that the transport proposal says do not belong there. Search-before-write therefore found nothing to build on.
- `sync_state`, once, with a real description: a 5.6 KB `compact` response, `action: proceed`, `verdict_confidence: provisional`, `evidence_basis: ode_fallback`, `corroboration: claim_only`, and a freshly minted `prediction_id`. Every caveat was honest. Nothing in the response changed what the session did next.

The answer: not voluntarily, for a task shaped like this one, where the transcript and the pull request are already the record. The session would use it when (1) the harness threads identity and check-ins without prose (the plugin does this); (2) start-of-session retrieval surfaces prior findings about the files and task at hand, which requires a knowledge graph of findings rather than messages and a relevance floor that refuses to pad; (3) a reviewer's conditions can actually reach a running session, which today happens only through check-in refusals; and (4) the session is the successor of a process that used it, which is the only moment the record pays the agent rather than the operator. Under the shared contract the session is instructed to use it, and instructed use is not preference; the repository's own measurement rule ("voluntary usage is not a measurable quantity") applies to this answer too. Per the contract, one finding recording this review's disagreements was stored after the search found no equivalent.

### Is the proposed 30-day test fair

Fair in intent, unfair or underpowered in seven specifics.

1. **The control arm is artisanal.** "A structured handoff" is not the standard stack; most operators write none. The standard stack is Git, the pull-request thread, CI, and the harness's own session transcript, which a cold successor actually receives (Claude Code `--resume`, Codex session logs, GitHub's commit-to-session-log link **[unverified]**). A hand-written handoff imports its author's capture effort into the control arm and turns the test into a test of handoff-writing. The repository's own rehearsal already flags this: "a handoff written in advance necessarily contains what its author put in it."
2. **Capture and retrieval are conflated.** "Frozen incident packets with the same underlying source artifacts in both arms" equalizes inputs and tests retrieval only. The wedge's value is in capture: whether the objection, condition, or correction was recorded at all during real work. The rehearsal design in `docs/evaluations/accountability-journey/result-v0.md` separates stage 1 (let both paths record naturally, score against an oracle) from stage 2 (retrieval under equal facts). Run both and report them separately; the stage-2 rehearsal already tied 1.000 to 1.000.
3. **The decision rule is a kill rule on three incidents with a conjunction.** Any one miss "freezes the server as a research artifact." The measurement rule the audit cites says a fair zero retires the lever, not the goal; the rule as written retires the goal. Route failure to the next lever (a capture-quality fix, a cheaper bundle) and freeze only on two consecutive lever failures.
4. **It does not test the wedge as stated.** Conditions enforcement and outcome binding are excluded; only reconstruction is scored. Add a measure: did a reviewer condition or a recorded outcome reach the successor's decision. Measure 7 gestures at this and stops short.
5. **Blinding is impossible without the bundle.** A reader who sees a UNITARES export knows which arm they are reading. The neutral rendering the test needs is the portable bundle from action 3, which makes that action a prerequisite of the test, not a parallel task.
6. **Feasibility.** Two non-maintainer operators in 30 days, when the enrollment ledger has been empty since the protocol registered. The audit allows cold readers as a fallback; say so in the design, and say that cold readers may be models from a vendor other than the incident's agents, which are cheap, reproducible, and blind-able, with a human spot check.
7. **It duplicates a registered protocol.** The accountable-coordination ablation (#2249, registered 2026-09-15) already defines contrast C minus B as "durable memory and structured handoff." The 30-day test is a lighter discovery gate. That is fine if it is labelled a pilot under that umbrella; otherwise it is a second single-writer surface on the same question.

### What the audit missed

Ranked by consequence for a product whose only remaining bet is the claim, challenge, and outcome record.

1. **The harness vendors, absent from the competitor list.** GitHub Agent HQ (public preview Feb 2026; commit-to-session-log links Mar 2026; agentic audit-log events with agent session id and initiating human), Anthropic's Claude Managed Agents (server-side event history, fully fetchable; public beta Apr 2026), Claude Code's own transcripts, hooks, and memory, OpenAI Frontier (Feb 2026) and Codex's compliance API. All **[unverified]** except the Claude Code and Managed Agents documentation, which was opened. The trace and attribution half of the record is being absorbed into each harness, per harness, on the vendor's retention terms. The defensible surface is the cross-harness claim, challenge, and outcome layer; the audit never tested it against these.
2. **The transcript as the reconstruction baseline.** A cold successor already gets the predecessor's session transcript for free from every harness named above. Any test of reconstruction that does not put the transcript in the control arm is testing against a weaker baseline than the one adopters have.
3. **Memory services with revision chains.** Vertex AI Memory Bank revisions, Zep's bi-temporal invalidation, Mem0's history log, AgentCore Memory's operator-supplied metadata **[unverified]**. These are the closest live competitors to "durable claims with corrections." What they lack, process-attributed writes with lineage and epistemic labels, is the actual differentiator, and it must be stated that narrowly.
4. **Annotation queues in self-hostable evaluation platforms** (Langfuse, open source; Braintrust hybrid; LangSmith enterprise) **[unverified]**. A named reviewer scoring and commenting on an agent output is the nearest review record. The dialectic must demonstrably encode objections and enforced conditions or it is a heavier annotation queue.
5. **IETF SCITT and the audit-trail drafts.** SCITT (signed statements, transparency service, receipts, append-only log; draft-22) is the standards-track shape closest to durable attributed claims; `draft-sharif-agent-audit-trail` is the export shape #2256 already proposes **[unverified]**. A security reviewer will hold the record to these; adopt one or say why an operator-local record can be lighter.
6. **Demand.** The audit asks only what an adopter can choose. EU AI Act Article 12 and Article 26(6) logging obligations apply to high-risk systems, now from December 2027 after the Digital Omnibus deferral, and never covered ordinary coding-agent operators; ISO/IEC 42001 and NIST demand a log, not a claim record **[unverified]**. The regulatory driver is weak. This cuts against the product, and the audit should say it rather than leave it unexamined.
7. **The capture literature.** IBIS, gIBIS, QOC, and Compendium spent thirty years on disagreement-preserving design records; the retrospective concludes that nobody enters rationale for an unknown future reader without immediate value, and Grudin's disparity (those who record are not those who benefit) is the standard explanation of why such tools die. ADRs survived because they are lightweight, in-repo, and written by the decider at decision time. The audit measures burden (measure 6) and treats it as a judgment call; the literature treats it as the primary predictor of abandonment. The implication for this product: the check-in must return something the agent uses on its next turn, or the record stays as empty as the enrollment ledger.
8. **The in-repository platform investment** (about 42,400 lines of Elixir, the signed Wave 3 go-decision) is absent from the stop list.
9. **The customer assumption.** For ten months the customer has been the maintainer's own fleet. For a single-operator tool the survival question is whether it pays for itself there (handoff time saved, corrections caught before they cost), which is a different test and one the operator can run alone.
10. **The self-audit problem.** A governed agent audited the governor; so did this review; so did #2256. Three agent sessions reading the same documents on the same day and converging is not three independent readings.

### The strongest case for continuing

- The vendor-neutral, cross-harness record is a real gap, and the vendors have an incentive not to fill it: each wants the record inside its own harness, retained on its own terms, and one vendor-hosted evaluation record (OpenAI's Agent Builder and Evals) is being wound down within a year of launch **[unverified]**.
- Process identity with lineage as a write gate, and reviewer conditions that bind, are genuinely novel, already built, and cheap to keep.
- The evidence discipline is itself a moat of credibility. Few projects publish preregistrations, negative results, and a ledger of what their numbers do not establish.
- Ten months and 4.5 million events of continuous operation over a real heterogeneous fleet, plus a public dataset, are research assets nobody else has published in the open.
- The marginal cost of continuing narrowly is low: the record kernel (identity, knowledge, dialectic, observability handlers) is built and tested, and the expensive surfaces can be frozen rather than removed.
- There is observed value, even if dogfood: cross-vendor sessions coordinated through the record with no human relay.

### The strongest case for ending

- Ten months, one operator, zero external enrollments, seven registered protocols and none run. The project produces evidence apparatus about itself faster than evidence.
- The harness vendors are absorbing the surfaces with distribution this project will never have, and for code the pull-request thread already is the disagreement record.
- The one mechanism proven to actuate (pause) has no demonstrated benefit and was mostly suppressed; the estimator on the gating path is unvalidated; the pre-registered read is in December, and a non-detection there leaves the check-in loop with no evidence for its central inference.
- The maintenance burden is disproportionate to a record-keeping product: PostgreSQL with AGE and pgvector, Redis as the live session store, an Elixir lease plane, 14,000 tests, 68 migrations, 169 flags, one maintainer.
- Every deployment number is circular: agents building the system are the agents it measures.
- The simplest baseline (transcripts, pull-request threads, and a findings file in the repository) may capture most of the value at near-zero cost, and nobody has shown otherwise; the repository's own rehearsal tied.
- The regulatory driver that would have created outside demand is weaker and later than assumed.
- Opportunity cost: the same effort could produce a much smaller artifact (a claim, challenge, and outcome schema; an export format; a provenance lint) that others could adopt without running a server.
