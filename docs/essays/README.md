# Essays and optional intuition

These documents explore analogies and philosophical interpretations around
UNITARES. They are **non-normative**: they do not define API behavior, establish
scientific validity, or override the implementation and canonical contracts.

Use the [Reviewer Guide](../REVIEWER_GUIDE.md),
[EISV computation reference](../EISV_COMPUTATION.md),
[scope and threat model](../SCOPE_AND_THREAT_MODEL.md), and
[architecture](../UNIFIED_ARCHITECTURE.md) for evaluator-facing claims.

## How to use this section

Treat an essay as an explanation of why a design may feel intuitive, not as
evidence that the design works. Grant applications, investment materials,
research summaries, and technical evaluations should cite the canonical
references above and the dated evidence records they link. If an essay and the
implementation differ, the implementation and its tested contracts win.

The distinction is deliberate. A metaphor can help a reader form a mental
model, while also hiding assumptions or suggesting stronger conclusions than
the measurements support. Keeping these pieces in one visibly optional section
lets the project retain its reflective voice without making philosophy carry an
engineering or empirical claim. Future essays should name their limits, link to
the relevant normative source, and avoid presenting analogy as validation.

- [Tonality as an optional lens](tonality-metaphor.md) — a bounded analogy for
  self-relative measurement, with explicit points where the comparison stops.
- [Trajectory identity as a philosophy-of-mind reading](trajectory-identity-philosophy-of-mind.md)
  — a reflective interpretation of the continuity machinery, not an identity
