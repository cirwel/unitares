# KG embedding comparison — 2026-10-14 UTC

**Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B is the leading challenger by first-result ranking in
this pilot, but its advantage over EmbeddingGemma 2 is small and uncertain.
The larger opportunity is in the serving retrieval pipeline.** BGE-M3 itself
retrieves the known target in the top five on 47/48 cases under a simple exact
dense lookup, versus 28/48 through the live search service.

Classification: evaluation/tooling. No production search configuration,
database content, model registry or lifecycle policy was changed by this work.
The feature freeze permits this local experiment; it does not authorize a new
production model channel or a ranking-policy change.

## What was tested

[Design and reproduction](README.md), [fixed labels](labels.json),
[results](results_2026-10-14.json), [snapshot receipt](snapshot_2026-10-14.json).
At 04:03:28Z the read-only snapshot contained 2,073 discoveries; 701 remained
after the default archived/cold exclusions. It is private and stays local.
The labels were fixed before scoring: 24 purposively selected actual logged
searches and one agent-authored paraphrase per topic. All 24 targets were
in scope and their summaries inspected. This is a known-item pilot, not a
random sample, exhaustive relevance labeling or a blind independent judgment.

All three models saw the same summary + first 6,000 details characters, with
their documented prompts, normalized vectors and exact cosine retrieval.
All document vectors, including BGE's, were freshly computed. The models ran
sequentially on the same M4 Max / 128 GiB host using MPS bfloat16. Revisions,
library versions, hashes and timings are in the results JSON. Gemma's probe
confirmed the text-only model had 271,002,624 parameters.

## Encoder results

Counts below mean **the pinned known target**, not every potentially correct
answer. MRR@10 rewards finding it earlier and gives zero beyond rank ten.

| BGE-M3 | 37/48 | 47/48 | 47/48 | 0.853 | 13.8 / 62.3 ms |

Encoding all 701 documents took 62.8 seconds for BGE, 122.6 for Qwen and 91.7
for Gemma. Query timing includes tokenization, encoding and vector transfer,
with synchronization and two warm passes. It excludes search, transport and
serialization, so it must not be compared directly with MCP latency. This is
one sequential run per encoder, not a load or endurance test.

| Previously logged | 0.819 | 0.918 | 0.931 | 0.924 |

The paired Qwen–Gemma MRR@10 difference is +0.0169. An exploratory paired
bootstrap over **24 topics**, preserving each query pair (10,000 resamples,
NumPy seed 20261014), gives a 95% percentile interval of [-0.0283, +0.0660].
It spans zero. Qwen wins four individual queries, ties 42 and loses two;
this does not establish a generally superior model. The bootstrap describes
variation inside this selected sample, not uncertainty over all KG usage.

One revealing label limitation: Qwen misses the exact correction record for
the confidence-schema paraphrase (rank 17), but its first result is the earlier
note explaining the same schema mismatch. It is counted as a miss by the
frozen target label, even though it contains relevant information. Labels were
**not** revised after seeing this result. Gemma places that target at 23;
Qwen's other top-five miss is the freeze clarification (rank 7), while BGE
puts that target at 22. These cases belong in a subsequent relevance review.

## Fusion and the live reference

The fixed RRF comparisons isolate first-stage fusion; they omit AGE
connectivity, age/status weights, authority ordering and response formatting.

| Encoder | Dense MRR@10 | Hybrid + AND | Hybrid + OR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| BGE | 0.853 | 0.788 | 0.775 |
| Qwen | 0.916 | 0.826 | 0.800 |
| Gemma | 0.899 | 0.810 | 0.764 |

Twenty of 48 frozen queries have no AND lexical match. Nevertheless, changing
everything to OR is not supported by this pilot: lexical-only MRR falls from
0.429 to 0.367, and every encoder's OR fusion ranks targets worse than its AND
fusion. OR does raise lexical recall@20 from 34/48 to 40/48. This is a
precision/recall tradeoff, not evidence that keyword search should be removed.
The fixed 0.3 dense floor produced the same target metrics as unfloored AND
fusion here; this does not calibrate that floor for another corpus or model.

A [live reference](live_reference_2026-10-14.json) replayed the same 48 queries
through `search_shared_memory`, limit 20, full response with details disabled.
All succeeded; 22 reported hybrid RRF and 26 semantic mode. The known target
was first on 19/48, top-five on 28/48, and top-ten on 37/48 (MRR@10 0.486).
The top-five split was 20/24 logged and 8/24 paraphrased. Requests were batched
three at a time; their timings are not a serial latency benchmark.

This is an **observational serving reference**, not an exact ablation: it
uses the live mutable corpus, existing stored vectors, candidate filtering and
the deployed age/connectivity/status/authority policies. A health read after
the replay reported build `54a282044`, started before the replay. A separate
concurrent operator/session deployment occurred during this work; this session
did not deploy or restart the service.

An exploratory [stored-vector control](stored_control_2026-10-14.json), added
after the fresh BGE result, reused the frozen production document vectors with
the same freshly encoded BGE queries. Exact dense top-five remained 46/48
(MRR@10 0.845). Thus the observed live gap is not accounted for simply by
re-embedding the documents. This control still does not isolate the particular
serving policy responsible; candidate scope, approximate retrieval, scoring
and mode routing remain confounded.

## Memory and integration observations

Process peak RSS was 3.98 GiB BGE, 1.26 GiB Qwen and 1.18 GiB Gemma. RSS alone
does not represent total unified/GPU memory. End-of-run MPS driver allocation
was 2.10, 5.17 and **52.87 GiB**, respectively. That is not a minimum model RAM
requirement or a measured peak. Gemma's allocation deserves investigation
before deployment.

A separate Gemma memory probe confirmed 0.505 GiB active model allocation;
a long eight-document batch left 5.21 GiB driver allocation, falling to
1.65 GiB after `torch.mps.empty_cache()`. These observations show that the
driver figure includes reclaimable allocation; they do not explain all of
the 52.87 GiB full-run figure. Raw probe measurements are preserved alongside
the report. The probe did not alter quality scores or rerun their labels.

Qwen works with the model family already supported by the production-era
libraries. Gemma 2 required an isolated newer environment here: transformers
5.19.0 and sentence-transformers 6.1.0, plus Pillow, torchvision and
sentencepiece. All three scored runs used the same torch/transformers/
sentence-transformers versions; the extra processor dependencies were added
only to this environment. Both models were downloaded with pinned revisions;
no remote inference or custom repository code was used.

## Recommended next work

1. Keep Qwen as the leading model challenger and Gemma as a close alternative.
   Preserve BGE as the baseline. Do not migrate on this small point difference.
2. Run a frozen **serving-path** ablation with current BGE: candidate visibility
   before versus after pool truncation, semantic score before/after age/status/
   connectivity weighting, mode routing, then authority ordering. The current
   results justify that diagnostic; they do not yet identify a specific bug.
3. Validate the failure cases with broader relevance judgments, including the
   earlier schema note, current-versus-superseded claims and no-answer queries.
   Keep these original labels as the fixed pilot instead of silently improving
   them. Add unseen topics before choosing a production model.
4. Evaluate late corrections/chunking separately. A better encoder cannot see
   text omitted by the 6,000-character window. Preserve the authority and
   lifecycle contract while testing how current corrections become retrievable.

The critique's digest complaint also needs its chronology: PR
[#2767](https://github.com/cirwel/unitares/pull/2767) merged October 13 at
09:02:54Z and adds bounded rows beyond the first three for an explicit limit.
The later critique still reports the older experience. Verify caller response
mode and deployment before proposing the same response fix again. The critique
is useful evidence of an agent's experience, not a verdict that every mechanism
it mentions is absent or broken.
